Forward churn

A cohort curve follows one intake as it decays. These follow the whole base from a standing start: take everyone active in a month, and see how many are still there four months later. That is the number that moves revenue, and it can be asked of every month rather than only of new arrivals.

What was decided, and what is still estimated

Three allocations used to be adjustable here. They are settled now, and applied in the pipeline rather than on this page, so the numbers above arrive already carrying them.

Customer Success
0% of acquisition cost. It is retention work, including its commissions, which are paid on retention. Counting it as acquisition overstated acquisition cost by $1.87m across the period, about 22%.
Partnerships
100% of acquisition cost. It sources customers.
Technical Account Manager
Cost of sales, not acquisition. $1.06m, carried in the open bucket and excluded from every cost figure on this page.

There is no control for these because deciding was the point. A slider invites a reader to move a number until they like the answer. The arithmetic is still parameterised in the code, so a different decision is a one line change rather than a rebuild.

Three of the charts above have a dimension that will not fit in one frame. These hold both axes fixed and move only the thing being varied, so a point that shifts has really shifted rather than being rescaled. They are redrawn from the data on every push, so they cannot fall behind the page.

What acquisition costs

Every line the pipeline counts as acquisition, by month, with the logo count and the cost per logo underneath it. Partnerships is the one split line that reaches this total, at 100%; Customer Success is settled at 0% and Technical Account Manager sits in cost of sales, so neither appears.

What is solid and what is not

The logo counts, the flows and the retention curves come straight from the workbook and hold whatever anyone decides about cost allocation. The ratios do not. LTV:CAC and payback both move further on the Customer Success split than on every other correction combined, and that split is an open question rather than a measurement.

Two limits worth carrying. Gross profit here is what a cohort has actually returned to date rather than a projection, so young cohorts are understated by construction. And departures are counted two ways: the push books a churn when it sees the transition, while a customer whose subscription drops out of the Stripe export simply stops appearing. The second count is about two thirds higher, and which one you use decides whether the base is growing or shrinking.
