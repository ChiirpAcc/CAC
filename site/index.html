Definitions

Each term is defined once here, with the charts that use it. Where two charts answer a similar question differently, both are named, so a reader comparing them knows why the numbers differ.

Contribution
Revenue less platform, people and variable cost, per account. Charts 36 and 49; the Events and Marketing tabs.
Gross profit
Revenue times one assumed margin. Charts 1, 2, 3 and 17. Chart 33 is the same question with the margin removed and every cost shown.
Cost per new logo
All acquisition spend, payroll included, over new logos in the month. Charts 1, 2, 7, 17, 26, 27, 33, 47. The Marketing tab’s per-customer figures divide direct channel spend only, and are not comparable.
New customer
By cohort, the month of first payment, in every chart except 10, which uses the summary’s first appearance.
Active and paying
Active is present on the books, including $0 accounts; paying is above $0. Costs per customer use paying (39, 49) or active (29, 40); revenue per customer uses active with never-paid accounts left out (28, 42).
Retention
Logo retention is the share of an intake still present; revenue retention is capped gross revenue retention. Both are indexed to the second month. Charts 4 to 12 and 44 to 46.
Churn
Present one month and absent the next (chart 5); by tenure (31); or forward, as the share of a month’s base gone later (18 to 25). A $0 month between two paid months is not churn.
Price
What a customer pays from their second month. Charts 13 and 14.

1. Is a customer worth what they cost?

The headline. Each month of new customers, followed for the same number of months, set against what it cost to win them. Chart 1 is the ratio, 2 and 3 are when the cost comes back, 17 is the same as a table, and 33 takes the assumed margin away and shows every cost.

Definitions in this section

  • Value: gross profit at an assumed margin. Revenue times a margin set once for the whole page, not measured per customer. Charts 1, 2, 3, 17.
  • Value: revenue, with every cost shown. No margin assumed; each cost group sits in the denominator and can be switched off. Charts 33.
  • Cost to win: cost per new logo. Acquisition spend in the month over new logos that month, as section 2 defines it. Charts 1, 2, 17, 33.
  • New customer: by cohort. Counted in the month of their first payment. Charts 1, 2, 3, 17, 33.
  • Same age. Every cohort is cut at the same age; younger ones are projected and drawn hatched. Charts 1, 2.

2. Winning customers

What acquisition costs, line by line, how many customers it bought, and what they pay. For where customers came from, by channel and by event, see the Marketing and Events tabs, which use a narrower spend measure.

Definitions in this section

  • Cost per new logo. Every 6100 line except revenue share, plus every 6150 payroll line except Customer Success. Partnerships counts 100%, Customer Success 0%, Technical Account Manager sits in cost of sales. Charts 7, 26, 27.
  • New customer: by cohort. First payment month. Charts 7, 26, 27.
  • New customer: first appearance. The summary tab’s count, which can differ from the cohort count in a month. Charts 10.
  • Price at signup. What a customer pays from their second month, so the joining charge booked as MRR until mid-2025 is not read as price. Charts 13, 14.
  • Not the same as the Marketing tab. Marketing divides only direct channel spend (ads, sponsorships, event fees) by paying customers per source, with no payroll, so its per-customer figures are far lower. Charts 26, 27.
  • How sure. Costs from September 2025 to July 2026 tie to QuickBooks to the dollar; August 2026 has not closed; months before September 2025 were never checked and the ledger then had fewer accounts, so older cost per logo is likely understated. Charts 7, 26, 27.

3. Keeping customers

How long customers stay and how much of their revenue stays with them. Charts 4 to 12 follow intakes by age; 31 cuts the standing book by tenure; 44 to 46 are revenue; 18 and 19 follow the whole base forward from any month.

Definitions in this section

  • Logo retention. Share of an intake still present, indexed to month 1 (the second month). Charts 4, 6, 8, 11.
  • Revenue retention. Capped gross revenue retention: what an intake still pays, no customer counted above their starting amount, indexed to month 1. Charts 4, 9, 12, 44, 45, 46.
  • Logo churn. Present one month, absent the next (the solid line); the summary’s churn over its prior base is drawn dashed beside it. Charts 5.
  • Churn by tenure. The standing book, cut at 3, 6 and 12 months since first presence. Charts 31.
  • Forward survival. Everyone active in a month, followed forward as one fixed set: a different question from cohort retention. Charts 18, 19.
  • Not a departure. A month at $0 with revenue on both sides (a late payment or a coupon) keeps the customer present. Charts all.

6. What it costs to keep them

What serving a customer costs, by layer and by account, and the price below which a customer costs more than they bring. Acquisition is left out here; it belongs to section 2.

Definitions in this section

  • Cost to keep one customer. The month’s spend in five layers (platform, people, variable, G&A, R&D) over paying logos. Charts 39, 49.
  • Over active logos instead. Charts 29 and 40 divide by every active logo, so they read lower than 39 and 49 for the same month. Charts 29, 40.
  • Your own definition. Chart 42 lets you pick the layers and the customers; a blend of the last six and three months. Charts 42.
  • Contribution. Revenue less platform, people and variable cost, per account. This is the measure the Events and Marketing tabs use for what a customer returned. Charts 49.
  • Two price floors. Marginal: what one more or fewer customer changes. Allocated: every fixed cost spread over payers. Chart 37 shows both; confusing them is the expensive mistake. Charts 37, 38.

What was decided, and what is still estimated

Three allocations used to be adjustable here. They are settled now, and applied in the pipeline rather than on this page, so the numbers above arrive already carrying them.

Customer Success
0% of acquisition cost. It is retention work, including its commissions, which are paid on retention. Counting it as acquisition overstated acquisition cost by across the period, of it.
Partnerships
100% of acquisition cost. It sources customers.
Technical Account Manager
Cost of sales, not acquisition. , booked to cost of sales and excluded from every acquisition figure on this page.

There is no control for these because deciding was the point. A slider invites a reader to move a number until they like the answer. The arithmetic is still parameterised in the code, so a different decision is a one line change rather than a rebuild.

What each event cost, and what its customers have paid since

One row per event. Cost is the Event Costs tab, sponsorship plus travel, with disputed fees settled on the evidence below and a fee that paid for several events split across them. The customers are the ones whose lead source names that event, matched to Stripe. The lead source is the earliest relationship at the company, a deal tag before a contact tag before a campaign, and never dated before the event it names. A customer counts only if their first payment came in or after the event's month.

Every event number here is a floor. Nothing sets the lead source automatically: about one deal in five carries one, and the share swings with whoever was typing. A customer an event brought who was never tagged is invisible on this tab and stays invisible.

What each way of winning customers cost, month by month

Spend by category against the leads, deals and paying customers each produced, so a month of paid social can be read beside a month of events.

Accounts worth a conversation

Every active account paying something under $500 a month, named, with the Stripe identifier to look them up. The accounts paying nothing are a different conversation, starting to pay at all rather than paying more, so they are in the download and not in the table.

What is solid and what is not

The logo counts, the flows and the retention curves come straight from the workbook and hold whatever anyone decides about cost allocation. The ratios do not. LTV:CAC and payback both move further on the Customer Success split than on every other correction combined, and that split is an open question rather than a measurement.

One limit worth carrying: gross profit here is what a cohort has actually returned to date rather than a projection, so young cohorts are understated by construction.

Departures are counted two ways, and this page uses one of them on purpose. The push books a churn when it sees the transition; a customer whose subscription drops out of the Stripe export simply stops appearing and never generates one. Counting everyone who stops appearing finds about two thirds more. That is not an open question between two defensible numbers, because only one of them reconciles: over 2026 the booked count gives 311 out against 353 in, which would be a base growing by 42, while the base actually fell from 1,160 to 1,048. Counting everyone who stops appearing gives 493 out against 381 in, a fall of 112, which is exactly what happened. So the larger count is the rate and the booked one is a floor, and every churn figure on this page is the larger count unless it says otherwise.
